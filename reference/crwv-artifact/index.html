The other wall: cash CoreWeave must raise each year

Each year the plan needs cash for two things: building data centers and buying GPUs (capex), and repaying debt that comes due. Cash from operations pays for the green part; the red part has to come from new loans, bonds or shares. Street consensus plan.

Cash waterfall

Where a year of adj. EBITDA goes

Like a CLO payment waterfall: SPV lenders (A–C) are paid interest and amortization first from contract cash; what is left goes up to the parent for interest on D–F, then their principal. Where cash runs below zero, the rest has to be refinanced.

How this is built

Principal by year for amortizing loans is modeled: each facility's balance is spread over its life and scaled so the totals match the 10-Q schedule for Jun 30 debt — 2H26 —, 2027 —, 2028 —, 2029 —, 2030 —, after 2030 —. Notes, converts and the revolver are bullets at maturity.

Interest is the cash coupon on each month's balance, with SOFR held at —. Reported interest runs higher (discount amortization and new draws), so these payouts are a floor.

Today's debt only: undrawn commitments and future borrowing are left out of the wall. The funding chart shows the new capital the consensus plan needs; its interest is already inside consensus operating cash flow.

Scenarios: consensus EBITDA runs to 2028 and is held flat after. Backlog run-off recognizes only the — Jun 30 backlog on its disclosed timing, with no new contracts. Stress cuts that backlog for a top-customer shock.

The stack is an analogy: each SPV loan is secured by its own GPU pool and contracts rather than one shared collateral pool; converts rank equal to the notes legally and sit last here as the equity-linked layer.
